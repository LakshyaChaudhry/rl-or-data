# Pre-registration — fill §1–2 before the first RL run; §3 after Phase 1 evals; never after seeing test results

## 1. Hypotheses in my own words (Laksh)
- H1: Data affect - mixed difficulty is better training data. 1 - test whether mixed > easy for both RTF & GRPO, if yes then Bauer et al.'s advantage isn't RL unique
- H2: Implicit filtering: GRPO's advantage mostly comes from picking informative prompts. Give RFT the curated 'goldilocks' prompts where the base model gets 1-7/8 correct. IF RFT-Curated closes much of the gap to GRPO, then a large portion of RLVR's benefits is really just data selection/difficulty filtering
- H3: Genuine RL effect - after controlling data, does GRPO still win? - compare GRPO-curated vs. RFT-Curated with the same prompts and sampling budget - If GRPO still wins beyond seed noise, that residual advantage comes from things RFT lacks: negative samples, relative rewards, on-policy generation 
- What result would falsify each:
for H1: Falsified by an asymmetry: GRPO-Mixed > GRPO-Easy by more than seed spread, but RFT-Mixed ≈ RFT-Easy (or worse). That would mean the mixed-difficulty advantage only exists through the RL objective
for H2: Falsified if RFT-Curated ≈ RFT-Mixed — the same prompt filter that makes GRPO groups informative does nothing for SFT — while GRPO-Curated stays well above both. Also falsified if RFT-Curated is worse than RFT-Mixed
for H3: Falsified if the difference on test_300 is within 2× the pooled seed std, or it flips sign on ood_hard_200, or RFT-Curated ≥ GRPO-Curated. Any of those means: once data selection is controlled, the RL objective adds nothing you can measure at this budget.

## 2. Success criterion (copied from SPEC §10, restated)
- two bars that are both required:
    1) big enough gap on the main test set - here the gap in the greedy accuracy on the test_300 set should be larger than twice the typical seed-to-seed gap/wobble of the two pooled arms, as smaller gaps could be attributed to just random seed noise

## 5. Additional Notes to mention in Writeup
-   1. The chosen config may be somewhat lucky. Val has only 100 problems, so the "best" of 9 configs is partly noise. Test and ood numbers are still unbiased, because they're never used to choose anything. The write-up should say "best on val", not "optimal".
-   2. The tuning asymmetry is still there. RFT tries 9 configs per arm and GRPO runs Bauer's single fixed recipe. Keeping it this way is fine, but state it plainly in the limitations: it tilts toward RFT, so an H3 result in GRPO's favour is the more convincing one.
-   3. The overfitting guard only works through val. It catches configs that memorize the training prompts badly enough to hurt val. Held-out test and ood are what show real generalization.

## 6. Post-results registration: iterated RFT (2026-09-20; predictions written by Laksh; committed before any iterated-RFT sample exists)
- **What had been seen when this was written:** test_300 and ood_hard_200 results of all six arms, both controls and the reference models (results/phase4). This arm and its two contrasts are therefore **secondary, registered after unblinding**; they are not part of the confirmatory H1–H3 analysis.
- **Arm (fixed before running; details in tasks/06b):** frozen train_curated (73 prompts), seeds 1–3, 3 rounds × 64 samples per prompt = 14,016 rollouts; round 1 = first 64 per prompt of the existing base draw, rounds 2–3 sampled from the current policy; training rule per round: **continue the previous round's adapter on the new round's kept samples only**, fresh AdamW and cosine schedule each round (chosen by Laksh 2026-09-20 on the agent's recommendation; the alternative considered was restarting from base on the union of rounds); lr 1e-5, 4 epochs, no sweep (see §4); final = after round 3 regardless of the val curve; locked cap, prompt and extractor.
- **Contrasts, SPEC §10 criterion unchanged:** (i) IterRFT − RFT-Curated: what three policy refreshes of the sampler add within a positives-only SFT objective. (ii) GRPO-Curated − IterRFT: what remains. H3 = (ii) + (i) exactly. No threshold is set on any "fraction of the GRPO advantage recovered"; if such a fraction is reported it is descriptive only.
- **What (ii) does not isolate:** besides negative samples and group-relative advantages, GRPO still differs in 300 policy refreshes vs 3, one pass per sample vs 4 epochs, lr 5e-5 vs 1e-5, 19,200 vs 14,016 rollouts, and adaptive zero-advantage filtering. The arm is a "coarse policy refresh", not on-policy RL.
- **Asymmetric reading, fixed in advance:** if IterRFT closes most of the gap to GRPO-Curated, that is strong evidence that policy refresh explains GRPO's edge; if it does not, that only lower-bounds the on-policy effect, because offline RFT barely moves the policy at this learning rate.
- **Truncation:** the §7 flag applies to the new arm exactly as to the others. RFT-Curated's seed std omits sampling variance (one shared draw); IterRFT's includes it — stated next to the criterion.
- **Predictions (Laksh, in his own words, before running):**
  - IterRFT − RFT-Curated on test_300 (sign, rough size): small positive — a few points, maybe not past seed noise. Three refreshes of positives-only SFT should help some, but at lr 1e-5 offline RFT barely moves the policy, so I don't expect a big jump.
  - GRPO-Curated − IterRFT on test_300 (sign, rough size): still clearly positive; most of the H3 gap (GRPO-Curated − RFT-Curated) remains with GRPO. 3 refreshes ≠ 300, and IterRFT still has no negatives / relative reward — the residual should stay on the GRPO side.
  - Same signs on ood_hard_200? yes on both contrasts (same direction; sizes may shrink under truncation).
  - What result would change my mind about H3: if IterRFT closes most of the gap to GRPO-Curated (Δ within ~2× pooled seed std of zero, or flips), then what I called the on-policy / RL residual was mostly coarse sampler refresh, and H3 as an RL-objective claim is overstated. Also if IterRFT ≤ RFT-Curated on test — then even refresh does nothing for SFT at this budget, and the H3 residual is whatever GRPO does beyond refresh.
